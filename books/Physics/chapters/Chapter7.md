# Chapter 7 — Thermodynamics and Planetary Energy Balance

## 7.1 Track the energy of a gas parcel

For a parcel of fixed mass, write the first law as \(du=\delta q-P\,d(1/\rho)\), where \(u\) is internal energy per unit mass and \(\delta q\) is heat added per unit mass. A rising parcel expands as external pressure falls. If it exchanges little heat during the ascent, it cools by doing work. An ideal gas of constant heat capacities follows \(T\propto P^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}\) along this dry adiabat, with \(\gamma=c_p/c_v\).

If the surrounding air cools with height more rapidly than a displaced dry parcel, the parcel becomes warmer and less dense than its surroundings and can continue rising. Condensation changes the energy budget through latent heat. Atmospheric models use radiative transfer and convective stability together to determine where energy moves by radiation and where it is carried by overturning. ## 7.2 Derive the equilibrium temperature

A planet on a circular orbit intercepts stellar flux \(L_\star/(4\pi a^2)\) over its projected area \(\pi R_p^2\). Its Bond albedo \(A_B\) is the fraction of incoming power reflected to space across all wavelengths and directions. If absorbed power is reradiated uniformly over \(4\pi R_p^2\) with negligible internal heating, equating absorbed and emitted power gives

\[
T_{\rm eq}=\left[\frac{(1-A_B)L_\star}{16\pi\sigma a^2}\right]^{1/4}.
\]

**Worked example.** For solar irradiance \(1361\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\) and \(A_B=0.30\), the absorbed power per unit planetary surface area is \(238\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), giving \(T_{\rm eq}\approx255\) K. The result is an effective radiating temperature for this energy budget. A surface beneath an infrared-absorbing atmosphere can have a different temperature. Similarly, a planet that transports little energy to its nightside can have a much hotter dayside than the uniform-temperature calculation suggests.
